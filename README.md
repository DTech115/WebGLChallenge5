# Reflection Questions
## Which scene was the easiest to create?
Probably moonlight since it had the most basic positioning/colors & whole number values.
## Which scene looked the most realistic?
Campfire or moonlight's dark lighting feels the most realistic as it leaves more room for the imagination based off the shape graphics.
## How did ambient light affect the mood of each scene?
It would increase or decrease the overall "brightness" of the scene, more made it super bright & less made it super dark.
## How did light direction affect visibility?
Depending on the light's position, different parts of the scene would light up or dimmen. Of course, the lit up parts remained more visible.
## Which color produced the strongest visual effect?
"Darker" colors make more of a difference than super light ones like white or yellow for a bigger effect.
## Why do game developers spend so much time adjusting lighting?
Lighting is what makes things look "realistic" because it affects everything we see all the time. If you see game models in a vacuum without lighting, especially cell-shaded models, the style/realism completely vanishes & it looks weirdly, unnaturally bright.
## If you were making a horror game, what lighting would you use and why?
A very angular position with moodier colors & very low ambient light.
## Bonus Challenge

I thought using moodier colors, in this case blue, would make for a more interesting scene. The positioning is purposefully only hitting a little bit of the scene to maximize the effect of darkness in deep water.
